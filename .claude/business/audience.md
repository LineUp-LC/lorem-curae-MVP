# Target Audience

---

## Audience Sophistication

- **Awareness level**: Problem-aware to Solution-aware
- **Technical sophistication**: Beginner to Intermediate (digitally active, research-heavy about skincare)
- **Communication impact**: Lead with outcomes and emotional benefits, not technical specs. Use plain language, visual proof, before/after framing. Advanced users (estheticians, creators) want specs and integrations.
- **Selling impact**: Lead with education and trust-building. Show the journey: confused → guided → confident. For B2B creators, show monetization tools and audience access.

## Audience Segments

1. **Skincare Enthusiasts** — passionate about products, ingredients, and visible results
2. **Wellness Seekers** — value holistic health, routines, and self-care
3. **Beauty Entrepreneurs** — indie brands, estheticians, creators looking to grow
4. **Tech-Forward Consumers** — early adopters who trust AI, AR, and digital tools
5. **Price-Conscious Shoppers** — want trusted products at the best value

## B2C Segment (Primary)

- **Primary persona**: "The Overwhelmed Skincare Seeker"
- **Demographics**: Primarily female, inclusive of all genders. Age 18-45. Middle to upper-middle income. Urban/suburban, digitally active.
- **Psychographics**: Curious but cautious, empowerment-driven, skeptical of hype, science-interested, transparency-focused. Values self-improvement, holistic self-care, community belonging.
- **Daily frustration**: Cannot find reliable, personalized skincare guidance; overwhelmed by conflicting information; wasting money on products that don't work
- **Current solution**: Fragmented — Reddit/YouTube for education, Sephora/Amazon for shopping, separate apps for tracking, Instagram/TikTok for community
- **Why it fails**: No single platform connects discovery, education, tracking, and purchasing. No personalization depth. No progress accountability.
- **Buying triggers**: New breakout, frustration with routine, friend's transformation, starting self-care journey, seasonal skin changes
- **Objections**: "Do I need ANOTHER app?" / "Can I trust AI over a dermatologist?" / "Is the subscription worth it vs free Reddit advice?"
- **Online hangouts**: TikTok, Instagram, Reddit (r/SkincareAddiction, r/AsianBeauty), YouTube, skincare Discord, Facebook groups
- **Content consumed**: Hyram, Dr. Dray, James Welsh, Lab Muffin Beauty Science, The Ordinary community
- **Decision style**: Research-heavy, community-influenced, data-informed, intentional. Price-sensitive but quality-driven.

### Customer Insights

Curious but overwhelmed. Motivated by self-improvement. Skeptical of hype. Hungry for clarity, transparency, and personalization. Open to tech that simplifies. Seeking control over routine and results.

### Emotional Desires

Clarity over confusion. Visible progress. Feeling understood. Safety and reliability. Creative expression (sellers). Holistic self-care. Community belonging.

## B2B Segment (Secondary — Indie Creators & Estheticians)

- **Target**: Solo creators, micro-businesses (1-10 employees) — indie beauty brands, estheticians, skincare service providers
- **Persona**: Indie brand founder / solo esthetician
- **Pain point**: Lack of visibility, high marketplace fees, no built-in audience of engaged skincare consumers
- **Current solution**: Etsy, Instagram DMs, Shopify standalone, word-of-mouth
- **Why it fails**: No targeted audience, high fees, no trust infrastructure, no skincare-outcome analytics
- **Buying triggers**: Looking for distribution, launching products, wanting consumer data
- **Objections**: "Enough buyers?" / "Fees vs Etsy/Shopify?" / "Platform credibility?"
- **Sales cycle**: Short (self-serve onboarding)
